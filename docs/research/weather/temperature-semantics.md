## Executive finding and evidence scope

A **conditional, preregisterable design** is defensible for comparing two delivered near-surface forecast products against a qualified station-reference construct. An identical-height, identical-instant comparison is **not established**. GFS's candidate 2 m quantity, UKV's 1.5 m screen diagnostic and typical Met Office 1.25 m thermometers differ. Actual station histories and message-specific temporal support remain prerequisites. The design is **not fully preregistered and not executable as an approved experiment**: parameters and gates below must close before errors are examined. No forecast-skill, model-superiority, outdoor-suitability or Weather-readiness conclusion follows.

Evidence labels retain WR002 meanings: **CONFIRMED** means stated in the cited product/standard documentation, not checked in actual selected files; **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED** means some necessary attributes are documented; **INFERRED** denotes a reasoned proposed method; **UNRESOLVED** lacks required evidence; **CONTRADICTED** identifies a conflicting assumption. Protocol choices are Meridian proposals, not claims of mandatory WMO practice. Dated primary references and retrieval limits are in [the WR003 source ledger](sources.md#wr003-temperature-and-verification-evidence).

The [Met Office instrument explanation](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/guides/observations/how-we-measure-temperature) describes PRT thermometers at **1.25 m**, shielded and naturally ventilated. This is a network-practice reference, not a substitute for every selected station's 2025 metadata. Nominal diagnostic height above model ground is different from elevation above sea level and from actual sensor height above station ground. Model surface elevation, station elevation, vertical reference and their difference must be recorded separately; a source altitude with unknown datum stays qualified.

## Quantity compatibility decisions

| Pairing | Classification / conditions |
|---|---|
| Same air-temperature quantity/support represented in K and °C | **COMPARABLE WITH QUALIFIED TRANSFORMATION**: `T_C = T_K − 273.15`, exact unit offset; retain original units, missing mask and source precision. Differences in K and °C have the same numerical magnitude; missing sentinels must never be converted into temperatures |
| Same documented sensor construct/support versus another record of that construct | **DIRECTLY COMPARABLE UNDER STATED CONDITIONS** only after matching measurement time/interval, site identity, units and QC. This is a decision rule, not an assertion that these candidate files satisfy it |
| GFS 2 m or UKV 1.5 m versus a documented 1.25 m station value | **NOT DIRECTLY COMPARABLE** as an identical-height physical measurement. A separately declared **qualified station-proxy evaluation** may compare each delivered forecast to the same reference after time/QC/support gates, without claiming the heights are equivalent. No height conversion is established here |
| Actual GFS/UKV versus selected MIDAS records before history/time/QC closure | **UNRESOLVED** for the proposed experiment; source labels alone do not justify even the proxy evaluation |
| Instantaneous forecast versus hourly-mean temperature | **NOT DIRECTLY COMPARABLE**. An explicitly averaged forecast with matching interval could be a different qualified experiment if cadence and method are justified; sparse samples do not prove an exact interval mean |
| Instantaneous air temperature versus daily/hourly minimum/maximum, skin/soil/grass temperature, dew point or apparent temperature | **NOT DIRECTLY COMPARABLE**. These are distinct quantities/supports, not aliases |
| Heights/elevations adjusted by a standard lapse rate | **UNRESOLVED**, excluded from primary analysis. A formula can produce an adjusted number without producing a valid site forecast |

The scientific target is the **documented eligible station measurement**, not a reconstructed universal 2 m truth. Forecast–reference discrepancy includes model error, measurement error and mismatch of spatial, vertical and temporal support. The [ECMWF representativeness study](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/elibrary/81173-accounting-representativeness-verification-ensemble-forecasts) (Ben Bouallègue, June 2020, TM 865) demonstrates the importance of point/area mismatch in its ensemble experiments. It does not supply a correction or uncertainty variance for this UK cohort.

## Physical interpretation and transformations

Near-surface diagnostics depend on surface exchange, stability and model land/orography; they are not necessarily prognostic model-level temperatures. Keep any producer-described diagnostic or post-processing method and version. Snow, roughness/land use, urban heat, coastal mixtures, shading and stable boundary-layer inversions can change local temperatures and vertical gradients. Fine grid spacing does not remove these uncertainties. These considerations are foundational [W01/W03/W04/W11/W24](research-register.md), not new Meridian measurements.

Primary analysis uses delivered temperature without calibration, altitude adjustment, clipping extreme errors or reconstructed missing values. The model–station altitude difference is a descriptor, not an automatic exclusion or correction. If later evidence justifies a lapse-rate sensitivity, preregister its gradient source, sign, units, reference surface, validity regime and failure handling; compute it as a separate derived series on the same pairs. A constant environmental lapse rate assumes vertical structure that may fail in inversions, snow and shallow stable layers. It must not be tuned to the study errors or used to normalise 1.25/1.5/2 m screen-height differences. No such experiment is authorised here.

## Temporal identity and matching gate

Keep forecast **reference/initialisation**, source **issue/release**, acquisition, lead and valid time distinct. Forecast valid time must agree with reference plus decoded forecast step, with any statistical interval separately represented. GRIB fixed-surface type 103 denotes height above ground, unlike type 102 altitude above mean sea level. Product template 4.0 denotes a point-in-time analysis/forecast; it is a template meaning, not proof the selected GFS message uses it. NetCDF time coordinates/calendar, reference time, forecast period, bounds and `cell_methods` require inspection; absence of a familiar name is not proof of instantaneous support. [GRIB surface table](https://codes.ecmwf.int/grib/format/grib2/ctables/4/5/), [template 4.0](https://codes.ecmwf.int/grib/format/grib2/templates/4/0/), [released CF 1.11, §§4.4 and 7.3](https://cfconventions.org/Data/cf-conventions/cf-conventions-1.11/cf-conventions.html).

**New WR003 prerequisite:** the [historical Met Office surface guide, §§2.4 and 4.1](https://artefacts.ceda.ac.uk/badc_datadocs/ukmo-midas/ukmo_guide.html) describes SYNOP observations completed at HH−10, whereas CDL observations are on the synoptic hour. This dated guidance does **not** prove a universal −10-minute correction in 2025 MIDAS Open. The actual relationship between stored `ob_time`, nominal report time, instrument sample/averaging interval and message convention must be resolved per eligible message and station era. GMT guidance does not justify British Summer Time conversion. UTC timestamps are retained throughout, with offsets/calendar explicit.

Primary preference: **exact physical-time support**, with any short instrument average explicitly qualified. Match documented effective observation time to forecast valid time, retain sample start/end and nominal time separately, and prohibit temporal interpolation or nearest-hour rounding in the primary rule. A known short mean is not mathematically instantaneous: accepting it as a local-time proxy needs a declared duration/offset and scientific rationale before preregistration. Those parameters are currently BLOCKED. An undocumented mean, unknown offset, ambiguous zone/calendar or conflicting reference-plus-lead fails matching rather than becoming a zero error.

If observations really represent 23:50 but forecasts only 00:00, the proposed +24/+48 exact-time cohort cannot simply include them. Options for a **newly approved protocol revision** are a restricted on-hour message cohort, or a documented nominal-time proxy with a fixed offset and limitations. Temporal interpolation of same-cycle forecasts would require bracketing messages, increases acquisition and changes support/effective lead; nearest-time selection adds displacement without constructing the interval. None is silently activated here. If no suitable cohort remains, stop the three-way design and retain only the semantic feasibility finding.

Daily summaries and extrema stay excluded; source “hour ending” conventions for one variable do not determine all variables in the same report. Delayed receipt affects availability, not the measured timestamp. Multiple forecasts at the same valid time remain different cycle/lead records. Do not replace a missing cycle with a later forecast or compare an issue time as though it were a valid time.

## Independence and scientific interpretation

| Dependency | What must be retained / defensible limit |
|---|---|
| Reference source | Station/network, edition and original reporting lineage; a different download website does not create independence |
| Reference processing | QC, revisions and any corrected/estimated readings; neighbour checks and undocumented edits may induce dependencies. Do not treat a gridded analysis as a station measurement |
| Forecast initialisation | Product/version assimilation configuration and, where accessible, station/time use. Later verifying observations can be useful despite the same station being assimilated earlier; do not assert independent initial states |
| Forecast development/tuning | Historic calibration/post-processing and development-period overlap; a retrospective operational file differs from a later hindcast |
| Evaluation/calibration | No tuning on the primary sample; new calibration requires independent training/validation separation and its own task |
| Global/regional lineage | UKV lateral-boundary producer/run and shared observations may correlate errors; an improvement cannot be attributed solely to resolution |

[Met Office assimilation documentation](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/blog/2025/what-is-data-assimilation-and-how-does-the-met-office-do-it) explains operational information reuse. Exact January 2025 station/cycle acceptance by GFS and UKV remains UNRESOLVED; no observation-use logs were accessed. The permitted later interpretation is **verification against a qualified operational observing network with incompletely resolved assimilation dependence**, not an independent truth experiment. If independence is essential to a revised question, missing use/tuning evidence blocks that claim and may require a different reference. WR003 establishes obligations, not those logs.

## Applicability beyond this first profile

Quantity/time/QC/source identities, paired equal-lead design and support/dependence auditing generalise to global and regional tracks. Exact heights, calendars, grids, boundary lineage, archive routes and licences do not. Alpine work needs new exposure/elevation histories and forecast overlap; sparse networks support station-conditional results rather than broad area claims. Another temperature diagnostic requires a new compatibility assessment. Ensembles require member/probability semantics and appropriate probabilistic scores; deterministic MAE alone is not their verification protocol. Other quantities require their own intervals/units/QC. One nocturnal UK temperature pilot cannot establish seasonal, daytime, mountain, global or regional skill generally, and cannot define Weather's full capability taxonomy.

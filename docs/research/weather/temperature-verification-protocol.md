# WR003: near-surface temperature verification protocol v0.1

Documentary design, **10 October 2026**. Starting checkpoint and scientific evidence are in [temperature semantics](temperature-semantics.md). **Status: conditional draft; not fully preregistered; acquisition and execution BLOCKED.** No data, decoding, forecast errors, confidence intervals or empirical conclusions were produced. All method choices below are proposals to fix before inspecting errors; product/reference meaning cannot be changed to make the protocol work. [Acquisition gates](temperature-acquisition-gates.md) define remaining prerequisites and the sole next task.

## Question, estimand and claim boundaries

Candidate question: **for a fixed, metadata-eligible UK-mainland station cohort and January 2025 00 UTC forecast initialisations, what is the paired difference in station-weighted absolute discrepancy of historical delivered GFS 0.25° 2 m and UKV ASDI 1.5 m air-temperature forecasts at +24 and +48 hours against documented, quality-controlled MIDAS Open v202607 near-surface station measurements?** Each product is evaluated against the same qualified observational target; different forecast heights and spatial supports remain part of the product comparison. This is not an identical-height forecast contest or a causal resolution experiment.

| Question | Evidence needed / WR003 position |
|---|---|
| A Correct decoding | Exact message/array metadata, independent reference decoder, units/grid/scanning/packing/missingness checks. Checks designed below, not executed |
| B Correct interpretation | Source-specific height, diagnostic, interval, clock, grid and observation constructs. Partially resolved documentation; key gates open |
| C Fair reference comparison | Fixed eligibility, common pairs/leads, support and dependence qualifications. Conditional method specified; actual compatible cohort unproven |
| D Predictive performance/skill | Separately authorised input acquisition and preregistered numerical experiment; skill relative to a declared baseline needs baseline evidence |
| E Outdoor usefulness | Specific terrain/exposure/user-decision and uncertainty validation; beyond this pilot, no hazard or safety inference |

The primary estimand is finite-cohort product discrepancy, conditional on shared available pairs. It is not area-weighted national skill, an independent sample of all weather, or performance conditional on a conveniently chosen regime. Only 00 UTC valid times occur at both proposed leads: this is a **nocturnal pilot**, especially relevant to stability/height mismatch, with no diurnal-cycle or seasonal claim. January is a WR002 documentary overlap proposal, not selected because of model errors or scientifically representative climate.

Before numerical work, every PROVISIONAL parameter must be fixed or explicitly removed, every BLOCKED prerequisite must close or terminate the relevant claim, and the immutable protocol must be reviewed. ## Matching, fairness and loss of pairs

1. Create the expected cycle/lead/station inventory from the fixed protocol. Retain original source/product/run identity and metadata alongside any converted value. Forecast reference plus step must equal valid time. UTC is used throughout; reject ambiguous zone/calendar/issue-time substitutions.
2. Establish message-specific physical measurement time and interval from metadata, not receipt stamp or an hourly filename. Apply [the semantic time gate](temperature-semantics.md#temporal-identity-and-matching-gate). Unknown offset/support fails primary eligibility. Known HH−10 observations are not silently assigned to HH:00; no universal correction or tolerance is adopted.
3. Require one accepted reference and both specified forecasts for each `(station-era, initialisation, lead, effective-valid-time)` primary triplet. Preserve overlapping valid times across +24/+48 as correlated records; leads are analysed separately, never pooled as independent samples.
4. Missing, corrupt, wrong-level, analysis-instead-of-forecast, incompatible-clock or failed-QC data removes that **triplet** from the product contrast and records reason/product. Never use another cycle, fill a gap or change lead. Report source-specific availability as well as shared-pair attrition; conditional complete-case results may conceal non-random outages.
5. Use the same station population, observation, valid time and lead for both models. Compute station/lead completeness against the **planned** denominator, not the number of available files. Apply preregistered `C_MIN/P_MIN/N_MIN` without looking at errors. Removing a station for one product removes it from that lead's paired estimand; report resulting cohort changes across leads explicitly.
6. Preserve suite/version boundaries. If a selected period crosses a documented change, split a prespecified version stratum or reject/revise the period before errors; do not average unspecified products into one model identity.

The scientific equal-lead comparison disregards operational release latency as a predictor selection rule but records it where documented. A latest-available operational comparison would require exact historic issue/arrival times and an independent selection rule; it is outside this protocol. UKV's documented 3–6 h latency does not mean a shorter scientific lead. Different physics, diagnostic height, delivery regridding, assimilation, land representation and boundary forcing all confound any explanation based solely on grid spacing.

## Spatial matching and representativeness

**Proposed primary:** nearest geometric point on each **delivered product grid**, using decoded geographic coordinates and an explicitly versioned geodesic distance/earth model. Select by coordinates before inspecting temperature or masks; if the selected point is unusable, reject the pair rather than searching for a farther replacement. Do not describe a delivered regular grid as the model's computational mesh. Record original index/scanning order, coordinate/CRS, selected coordinate, horizontal distance, model terrain/land mask, station elevation/height/exposure and differences. Tie resolution uses source-grid index order, not lowest temperature/error. Reject missing values and unusable/out-of-domain support; a coast/sea-mask conflict is audited and excluded under the preregistered eligibility rule rather than searching arbitrarily farther for an attractive land cell. No distance or altitude threshold is invented: if `DIST_MAX`/terrain exclusions are wanted, they must be justified and fixed before errors, otherwise mismatch is reported without a threshold claim.

Nearest-point sampling is a simple, independently checkable **engineering choice**, not a scientific claim of optimality. It avoids adding smoothing/height adjustment to the initial comparison but retains displacement and point/area mismatch. It is conditional on source meaning: if metadata establish a cell average, label comparison to that support rather than claim a point forecast. Native/model and delivered-grid transformation provenance must remain distinguishable.

**Limited sensitivity:** bilinear interpolation on the delivered coordinate grid when coordinates and grid mapping permit it; record four contributing indices/weights and interpolation CRS. Require four valid, compatible-support neighbours; no renormalisation around missing/sea cells. Analyse nearest and bilinear only on their common eligible subset, with subset baseline counts. Bilinear can smooth gradients and mix coast, land types or elevations; it need not improve accuracy. Higher-order interpolation, common-grid regridding, terrain-adjusted sampling and adaptive station-specific method selection are excluded. If grids are curvilinear/rotated or support differs from assumptions, stop and revise the method rather than treating latitude/longitude as a convenient planar raster.

No primary altitude correction. Optional strata describe elevation, model–station terrain mismatch, coastal/inland and urban/rural exposure only when documented metadata and sample size support prespecified definitions. Terrain complexity needs a reproducible existing descriptor or remains unknown; no new Atlas/data acquisition is implicit. With ≤10 stations, most stratified comparisons may be descriptive or omitted. Preserve metadata missingness; unknown urban/coastal status is not rural/inland.

## Station eligibility and observation QC

Source QC and experiment eligibility are different. [MIDAS Open guidance](https://help.ceda.ac.uk/article/4982-midas-open-user-guide) says the latest data state can include un-QC-ed values and requires element flags. [WH table documentation](https://artefacts.ceda.ac.uk/badc_datadocs/ukmo-midas/WH_Table.html) exposes `src_id`, `id`, `id_type`, `met_domain_name`, `version_num`, `rec_st_ind`, `ob_time`, `air_temperature`, `air_temperature_q`, `air_temperature_j` and receipt fields. Its older shorthand about version 1 being checked conflicts with the Open guide's explicit caveat; the `qc-version-1` directory and version alone cannot pass QC. Exact current release headers/dictionaries control interpretation. No accept/reject flag list is invented.

| Required record | Mandatory evidence / rejection rule |
|---|---|
| Station-era | Stable `src_id` and reporting-ID mapping; coordinates/CRS, elevation and qualified vertical reference, history valid through study, sensor height/screen/exposure or explicit documented network applicability |
| Instrument/history | Sampling/averaging practice and message mapping; known moves, replacement, calibration/exposure changes. Unresolved measurement time/height or unlocated relocation fails the strict cohort; a documented stable era may be retained |
| Observation | Exact edition/path/input identity, measurement/report/support times, original value/units/missing code, field QC/descriptors/state/revision/message and replacement history |
| Eligibility | Required temperature measurement and shared forecast support; preregistered completeness on planned valid slots. Dataset explicitly identifies commissioning `src_id=99999` for exclusion; do not extend that rule to unrelated IDs |
| Source QC | Accept only enumerated temperature-specific flags demonstrably suitable under edition dictionary; no QC-run, suspect/rejected, estimated/corrected values require explicit policy. Unknown flag meaning excludes the record from primary use |
| Duplicate/conflict | First collapse byte/semantically identical duplicates with all references retained. Choose a unique documented current replacement using source precedence/receipt history **within the pinned edition**, not the value closer to a forecast. Distinct unresolved reports/values at one effective time are quarantined, never averaged |
| Missingness | No imputation, zero substitution or interpolation. Record unavailable, missing, QC rejection and incompatibility separately; show counts by source/station/lead/day |

Exposure uncertainty is scientific information. The strict small cohort may be empty if station records do not supply mandatory information; that is a valid blocker. Relaxing to documented network practice with unknown station details would be a separately recorded, qualified revision, not an invisible assumption. Exact station selection/order, completeness thresholds and any spatial stratification remain preregistration gates. Select from metadata/QC/missingness only; archive a dated selection receipt before forecast errors. Do not drop valid unusual measurements because discrepancies are large. Experiment consistency checks flag implausibility for review under source rules, rather than “correcting” reference data using forecasts.

Exclusion audit: candidate record key; source edition/era; planned pair; original metadata/flag; rule/version; retained/rejected status and reason; replacement lineage; decision time and whether errors were available. Preserve rejected inventory, duplicates and denominator without unnecessarily publishing restricted raw observations. Staff/manual QC does not make an observation error-free.

## Verification metrics, aggregation and uncertainty

For model `m`, eligible station `s`, lead `h` and its shared eligible initialisations `I_sh`, define `e_msih = f_msih − o_sih` in °C. Positive bias is warm forecast discrepancy. Let `n_sh = |I_sh|`, and `S_h` be the fixed eligible station cohort after preregistered completeness filtering. For any loss `g`, `A_mh(g) = (1/|S_h|) Σ_s [(1/n_sh) Σ_i g(e_msih)]`. This weights stations equally, then their shared times equally; it defines performance for this cohort, **not equal geographical area**. Empty stations/denominators yield unavailable results, not zeros. Dense networks do not acquire more weight merely by offering more candidate records.

| Measure | Exact definition / role |
|---|---|
| **Primary contrast** | `Delta_h = A_UKV,h(abs) − A_GFS,h(abs)` separately for +24 and +48. Equivalently station-average of paired `d_sih = abs(e_UKV,sih) − abs(e_GFS,sih)`; negative means smaller UKV absolute discrepancy for this sample only |
| MAE | `A_mh(abs)`; interpretable typical absolute discrepancy, primary individual-product companion |
| Bias / mean error | `A_mh(identity)`; signed cancellation possible, diagnostic |
| RMSE | `sqrt(A_mh(square))`; squares weighted before root, **not** average of station RMSEs; emphasises larger discrepancies, diagnostic |
| Distribution | Station/lead signed-error and absolute-error empirical distributions, median absolute error, specified quantiles and full extremes. Aggregate quantiles use weights `1/(|S_h| n_sh)` and a preregistered quantile algorithm; no pooled unweighted median labelled station-balanced |
| Station/lead detail | Counts, gaps, station-specific metrics and paired contrasts, date sequence; season/subgroup only with adequate distinct data. January-only cannot estimate seasonal dependence |

The [WMO LC-DNV definitions](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/spaces/WLD/pages/97370550/Score%2Bdefinitions%2Band%2Brequirements) (6 March 2024) support weighted ME/RMSE/MAE, with equal observation weights in its exchange procedure. **Our equal-station policy differs deliberately** because the estimand is a small station cohort; this is not claimed to be a compliant LC-DNV score submission. [Gneiting, Making and Evaluating Point Forecasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.0902) explains that scoring choices target different predictive functionals. MAE is chosen for this descriptive product comparison, not because it proves one forecast's full uncertainty distribution superior.

Paired observations and errors share weather, stations, cycles and overlapping valid dates. Raw station-hours are not independent sample size. [Hamill (1999)](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/14/2/1520-0434_1999_014_0155_htfenp_2_0_co_2.xml) treats pairing and spatial dependence in precipitation verification; its indexed primary text motivates these principles, not an automatically transferable temperature block length. Full retrieval was blocked; no quantitative result is imported.

**Proposed uncertainty design:** resample contiguous blocks of initialisation days, jointly retaining both forecasts, references, all stations, both leads and missingness. Recalculate the station-balanced statistic on each replicate; do not redraw products or stations independently. This preserves observed cross-station covariance within blocks and targets temporal uncertainty conditional on the fixed cohort. It does **not** justify inference to unobserved UK stations. Circular/moving blocks add edge assumptions; alternatives are stationary blocks or an explicitly modelled correlated-error approach, but each needs adequate history and diagnostics. An IID station-hour bootstrap or ordinary independent-error t-test is unsuitable.

Before errors, fix block selection rule/length `L`, `B`, seed, interval coverage `GAMMA`, interval method, replicate-zero-denominator rule and minimum distinct blocks. Use independently justified synoptic dependence evidence or a prespecified nuisance-estimation rule and fixed block-length sensitivities, not whichever produces significance. Metadata/observation cadence alone cannot determine forecast-error correlation. **These values remain open; no confidence-interval or effective-sample-size claim is made.** With 31 cycles, a plausible dependence scale may leave too few blocks; then report descriptive results and explicitly withhold robust inference. Extending the period requires an independently justified new profile/access/resource gate, not chasing a desired result.

Both leads are primary estimands; a later confirmatory claim over either requires a preregistered simultaneous-interval/multiplicity rule or separate clearly bounded claims. Diagnostics/subgroups/sensitivities are exploratory unless declared otherwise; report every prespecified result, not only favourable ones. No numerical significance threshold or practical superiority margin is selected without purpose/power evidence. Leave-one-station-out summaries assess cohort sensitivity without treating stations as independent weather replications. Period sensitivity needs separately planned dates; a short winter pilot cannot stand in for seasons or extreme-event verification.

Absolute error comparison is distinct from **skill relative to a baseline**. A persistence diagnostic would use the same qualified station construct at initialisation, forecast unchanged to target, with exact time/support and lawful access; any missing baseline yields a separately labelled subset, not changed main pairs. No climatology fitted from the evaluation month, future observations or source-derived analysis is an independent baseline. Baseline choice/availability and an independent training period, if needed, must close before a skill-score claim.

## Preregistration, anti-bias and scientific acceptance

Freeze a versioned protocol record and its source terms/evidence before opening forecast errors: question/estimand; domain/period/stations/era; exact product editions/fields/height/support/version handling; cycles/leads; UTC/physical-time matching; grid/CRS/distance/ties/masks; QC/revision/duplicates; missingness/denominators/completeness; metrics/weights/quantile algorithm; uncertainty/multiplicity; baseline; fixed sensitivity list; exclusions; claim limits; approved resources and rights. Include explicit parameter values, input/metadata inventory identifiers, independent review and record hash/commit. A Git hash establishes record integrity, not external preregistration timing or trusted publication authenticity. Record who had access to errors; a future public timestamp/registry is a separate choice, not performed here.

Later changes require original rule, revised rule, reason, decision time, error visibility and effect on estimand/reporting. Label post-error changes exploratory; retain original results where scientifically possible and report failures. Pre-error access failures may justify another period/reference, but require a new frozen cohort before errors. No calibration, station ranking or method tuning on the primary evaluation sample.

Future acceptance concerns **valid method**, not winning model: lawful complete pinned inputs; independent decoding checks; support/time/QC interpretation closed; reproducible pairing/exclusion counts; no silent substitutes; exact formulas and qualified uncertainty; complete reporting, including negative results. Define numerical decoding tolerances from source packing/precision and reference tools, never from desired agreement. Visual agreement or close model outputs is insufficient. No numerical error cutoff determines scientific success here.

## Programme implication

WR003 resolves the structure of a bounded protocol but exposes a **reference-metadata gate before implementation**: physical time, short averaging, height/history and actual QC meanings can invalidate the apparent hourly match. Close that small documentary gate alongside exact historical archive identities/terms before acquisition is worth proposing. No need for another broad model catalogue or a new tile format. The wider global/regional, ensemble, observations, nowcast, ML/hybrid, mountains, uncertainty, engineering, offline and Atlas/Guide tracks remain in [the programme](research-programme.md); this pilot neither completes them nor selects a provider.
